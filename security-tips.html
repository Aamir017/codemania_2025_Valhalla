Phishing Security Tips

Learn how to protect yourself from phishing attacks with these expert security tips.

Tip of the Day

Test Your Knowledge

Check Sender Details

Always verify the sender's email address, not just the display name. Hover over or click on the sender's name to reveal the actual email address.

Email Safety

Beware of Urgent Requests

Be suspicious of emails that create a sense of urgency or threaten negative consequences if you don't take immediate action.

Email Safety

Scrutinize Attachments

Never open attachments from unknown senders. Be especially cautious of compressed files (.zip, .rar) and executable files (.exe, .bat, .scr).

Email Safety

Grammar and Spelling Errors

Professional organizations typically proofread their communications. Poor grammar, spelling mistakes, and unusual phrasing are often indicators of phishing attempts.

Email Safety

Hover Before You Click

Before clicking any link in an email, hover over it to preview the actual URL. If it looks suspicious or different from what you'd expect, don't click.

Email Safety

Check for HTTPS

Always verify that websites use HTTPS (look for the padlock icon) before entering any personal information or credentials, especially for financial sites.

Web Browsing

Verify Website URLs

Check the domain name carefully. Phishers often use lookalike domains with subtle misspellings (like "paypa1.com" instead of "paypal.com").

Web Browsing

Type URLs Directly

Instead of clicking links in emails, type the website address directly into your browser or use a bookmark you've previously created.

Web Browsing

Be Cautious with Downloads

Only download files from trusted sources. Be especially wary of "free" software that seems too good to be true.

Web Browsing

Watch Out for Deceptive Pop-ups

Be skeptical of pop-up windows claiming your computer is infected or that you've won a prize. Never call phone numbers or click buttons in these pop-ups.

Web Browsing

Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Create complex, unique passwords for each of your accounts. Consider using a password manager to generate and store them securely.

Account Security

Enable Two-Factor Authentication

Whenever possible, set up two-factor authentication (2FA) for your accounts. This adds an extra layer of security beyond just your password.

Account Security

Regular Financial Monitoring

Check your bank and credit card statements regularly for unauthorized transactions. Set up alerts for account activities when available.

Account Security

Use Strong Security Questions

Choose security questions with answers that aren't easily found on social media or through a simple search about you.

Account Security

Verify Friend Requests

Be cautious of duplicate friend requests from people already in your network. Contact them through another method to confirm before accepting.

Social Media

Review Privacy Settings

Regularly check and update your privacy settings on social media platforms to control what information is publicly visible.

Social Media

Beware of "Too Good to Be True" Offers

Be skeptical of contests, giveaways, or offers that seem overly generous or require you to provide personal information.

Social Media

Think Before You Share

Be careful about what personal information you share on social media. Details like your birthdate, home address, or vacation plans can be exploited by attackers.

Social Media

Verify Unusual Requests

If you receive an unusual request from a colleague or manager, especially involving money transfers or sensitive information, verify through another communication channel.

Workplace

Lock Your Computer

Always lock your computer when stepping away from your desk, even if it's just for a moment (Windows key + L or Command + Control + Q).

Workplace

Document Suspicious Activity

Report suspicious emails or security incidents to your IT department immediately. Take screenshots or save copies as evidence.

Workplace

Be Cautious on Public Wi-Fi

Avoid accessing sensitive work information when connected to public Wi-Fi networks. Use a VPN when working remotely.
